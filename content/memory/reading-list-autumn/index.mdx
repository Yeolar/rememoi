I keep a small list of things I have read and want to remember. It is not a review column; it is closer to a shelf of postcards. Here are five books and one essay that stayed longer than expected.

## Books

### *The Creative Act* — Rick Rubin

Less a manual than a set of reminders. The useful part is the insistence that attention is the work, and that the artist is mostly a person who keeps showing up.

### *Several Short Sentences About Writing* — Verlyn Klinkenborg

A book that argues against the sentence you were about to write. It made me slower and, I hope, a little clearer.

### *The Art of Noticing* — Rob Walker

Small exercises for paying attention in ordinary places. It is the kind of book that changes a walk to the corner shop.

### *A Pattern Language* — Christopher Alexander

Too long, too confident, and still full of useful questions about why spaces feel the way they do. Read it the way you would read a dictionary.

### *How to Do Nothing* — Jenny Odell

A useful counterweight to the idea that attention is a resource to be spent. The chapter on bioregionalism is worth the whole book.

## One essay

<Aside type='tip'>

“The Case for Slowness” by an author whose name I have unfortunately lost. If you know it, tell me.

</Aside>

The essay argues that slow reading is not a luxury but a form of resistance: a way of refusing to let the algorithm decide what deserves your time.

> Read the thing twice. The second reading is where the book begins.

## A note on lists

Lists like this are always partly a performance. I have not read everything on my shelf, and I abandon more books than I finish. The point is not to have read; the point is to have been changed a little by what you did read.
